# Playing to Wait: A Taxonomy of Idle Games
* Idle games are minimalist games with minimal interaction
* Players move from playing to planning
* Accumulate resources using a core mechanic such as clicking or tapping
* Most include automation mechanics
* Examining games temporally can reveal differences
* Idle games are distinct from more active games as they involve less active play
* Bots are a precursor to idle games
* Progress Quest was released in 2002
* Cow Clicker was released as a satire, and was popular anyway (2010)
* While graphics are nice, they're not required
* "Managing resource accumulation and spending is a critical part of game balance"
* "Balancing resource production rates and costs is a critical element of idle games"
* "...so the researchers engaged in multiple play sessions for each game..." - so you can play games for research. :)
* Idle games progress without player interaction
    * Majority background
    * Waiting *is* playing
    * Temporal flexibility, little to no penalty for leaving and coming back
    * One instance is played over a long time
    * Can't lose
* Incremental Games
    * Resource generation
    * Spend resources to generate more resources
    * Built around accumulated required resources for progress
* Sub-genres of Incremental games
    * Micromanagement Games
        * Generally increase complexity over time
        * Building an internal economy
        * New Game+/Prestige
    * Single Resource Games
        * Produce and spend a single resource
        * High level of interaction
    * Derivative Games
        * Produce generators which produce generators
        * Eventually still producing a single main resource
    * Multiplayer-Incremental
        * Allow multiple players to contribute to the same incremental game
* Spectrum of interactivity
    * Clickers are high interaction
    * Minimalist is low interaction
    * Zero-Player is no interaction
* Idle games are interesting
    * Encourage leaving and coming back later
    * Waiting is part of play
    * Resetting the game is rewarded
    * Fill 'microboredom' moments
    * Never not playing
* Incremental games incentivize Playing Less and Planning More
    * Plans span multiple play sessions
    * Can offer queue mechanics to plan future work
    * Games often provide estimates for time to complete
